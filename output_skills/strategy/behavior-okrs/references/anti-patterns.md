# Key Result Anti-Patterns

These illustrate the principle. Consider what fits your context.

Each pattern fails the same test: no target audience doing something differently. The fix is always to ask "what will [who] do differently if this works?"

## Contents
- Task or output
- System metric
- Team activity
- Vague
- Over-scoped or cascaded
- Vanity metric

## Task or output
"Launch the new portal." "Ship 5 reports." A deliverable, not a change. Ask who is meant to use it and what they will do once they have it. The behavior is the key result; the portal is a hypothesis for achieving it.

## System metric
"99.9% uptime." "Cut load time by 2s." Measures the system, not a person. Ask what the human does when the system is better (completes checkout, returns weekly). If nobody does anything differently, it is operational health: track it as business-as-usual, not as a key result.

## Team activity
"Run 20 customer interviews." "Design 10 ads." Describes what the team does. Move the subject from the team to the audience.

## Vague
"Improve satisfaction." "Drive engagement." No observable behavior. Replace with something a third party could count: renewals, repeat purchases, tasks completed without support contact.

## Over-scoped or cascaded
A team adopting company revenue as its key result. The team cannot move it alone. Narrow to the slice of the journey the team influences, and the behavior it can plausibly change.

## Vanity metric
Totals that only go up (page views, sign-ups, downloads) with no link to valuable behavior. Ask whether the number can rise while the audience's behavior stays unchanged. If yes, it is not a key result.
