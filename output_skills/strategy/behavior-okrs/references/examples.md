# Examples

These illustrate the principle. Consider what fits your context.

Each example is read through the Key Result Test: a target audience, doing something differently, by a measurable amount. Verdicts are this skill's judgment, not the book's. When an example is weak, say so rather than copying its shape.

## Strong examples

### Flying car company (company level)
Objective: Redefine local and regional air travel in North America to make it simpler, safer, and more accessible by the end of this decade.
- Deposit-paying customers sell out the waitlist for the first three model year releases. (Who: deposit-paying customers. Does what: commit money. How much: sold out.)
- Our operators cause no safety incidents during the first year of market rollout. (Behavior of operators, measured as zero.)
- At least 35% of local and regional travelers (air and land) regularly use our flying vehicles.

Marketing objective: Create broad awareness of the safety and sustainability promise of modern flying cars by the end of this year. Awareness is not yet a behavior; the key results for this objective must say what travelers *do* once aware (request a demo, join the waitlist, change their stated consideration set) and by how much.

### Internal customer: shoe design feedback
Objective: Make it easier for all necessary departments to provide design feedback by the end of the quarter.
- Brand managers provide feedback 50% faster on each shoe design.
- The manufacturer produces physical prototypes of each shoe design 100% faster.

The customers are internal colleagues. Both key results describe what those colleagues do, with a measurable change. Note "faster" needs a baseline (current days to feedback) before it is a usable target.

### Virtual healthcare: company and support team
- 100% of new enterprise customers are fully onboarded and using the product within 30 days of signing.
- Existing customers make referrals at a 25% higher rate than last year.
- Customers who had serious support issues renew their contracts at a 100% rate.
- Customers get answers through the online channel instead of the call center at least 75% of the time.
- Customers who contact support have the problem resolved on first contact 95% of the time.

Each has an identifiable audience and an observable action. Caution on "market leader" and "best customer experience" objectives: they are fine as aspirations only if the key results carry the measurable proof.

### Grocery checkout: team of teams
Objective: The simplest checkout process in the online grocery business by the end of 2025.
- Customers successfully complete checkout on first attempt (+75%).
- Customer checkout time is reduced by 50% on average.

Component teams (product page, sign-in, payments) each take a narrow slice with leading indicators: customers add to cart after a product page visit (+55%), failed authentication attempts during checkout drop 95%, carts abandoned for payment issues drop 90%. Each team stays inside its scope of control while the shared objective keeps them aligned.

## Mixed examples (good intent, needs sharpening)

### Best place to work
- Employees who would recommend us as a place to work increase 10%. Behavior-adjacent: a stated intent to recommend. Strengthen with what employees actually do (referrals made, offers accepted from referrals).
- Reduce regrettable turnover by 10%. Real behavior (people staying). Needs a definition of "regrettable" and a baseline.
- HR team: "All employees take 2 to 3 weeks of vacation each year." Employees are the audience and vacation-taking is observable. Check that the HR team controls the levers (policy, manager norms) and that the target is not a mandate that employees will game.

### Support team: "Customers speak to fully trained agents 100% of the time"
The subject is customers, but the measure is about the team's staffing. The behavior change is hidden. Ask what customers do when agents are better trained (resolve in one contact, stop re-contacting) and measure that instead.

## Weak examples and rewrites

### Sustainability: secure new gas sources
Objective: Become a more sustainable company in the near future. Key result: Secure two new sustainable sources of natural gas in the next year.
- Fails: it is a task completed by the company. No audience changes behavior. The objective also has no timeframe worth the name.
- Questions: who is affected by the company's sustainability (customers, regulators, plant operators, communities)? What do they do differently if the company is more sustainable?
- Direction (needs real data): Large buyers renew or increase orders citing sustainability criteria by X%; plant operators run lines on lower-emission fuel for Y% of production hours. Sourcing gas is then a hypothesis, not the key result.

### Volunteer matching platform
Key results: 100 volunteers in the system; 10 organizations in the system; 10 matches made; 1 project underway.
- Fails in part: the first two are counts of accounts (vanity metrics: they rise without anyone doing anything valuable). Matches are closer, but "made" by whom?
- Direction: Volunteers who sign up begin working with an organization within 30 days, at X%; organizations that post a need receive an accepted volunteer within 14 days, at Y%. Counts of sign-ups become leading indicators, not key results.

## Additional rewrites for common team types

### Data team
- Weak: "Publish 12 new dashboards."
- Rewrite: Account managers make renewal-risk decisions using the shared dashboard instead of ad hoc requests to analysts, cutting analyst requests by 60%.

### B2B SaaS onboarding
- Weak: "Reduce time-to-first-value in the product." (no audience, vague)
- Rewrite: Client admins invite their first five teammates within 7 days of signing, at 70% of new accounts (baseline unknown: instrument first).

### Security team
- Weak: "Complete the phishing training campaign."
- Rewrite: Employees report suspicious emails within one hour at least 80% of the time in simulated phishing tests. Training is a hypothesis.

### Legal or compliance team
- Weak: "Publish the new contract templates."
- Rewrite: Sales reps close standard-terms deals without a legal review request at least 70% of the time.

## Team Topologies: platform and other team types

In Team Topologies the customer of a platform, enabling, or complicated-subsystem team is the stream-aligned teams it serves. Success is what those teams do without depending on the serving team. Numbers are illustrative; each needs a baseline.

### Platform team (X-as-a-Service)
Objective: Stream-aligned teams reach production without thinking about infrastructure, by the end of Q3.
- Stream-aligned teams deploy to production without opening a ticket to the platform team in 90% of deployments.
- Developers of a new service make their first production deploy within one day of creating it, instead of two weeks.
- Teams choose the platform over building their own infrastructure for 80% of new services. Voluntary adoption shows the platform is a product that delivers value, not a mandate.

### Cognitive load
- Developers resolve a common infrastructure incident without escalating to the platform team in 70% of cases.
- Time stream-aligned teams spend on operational work outside their domain drops 30% (perception survey plus ticket data, since direct measurement is hard).

### Enabling team (facilitating mode)
Success is the other team no longer needing the enabling team.
- Coached teams apply the practice (e.g. contract testing) on their own in the following quarter, in 80% of cases.
- Coaching engagements end within six weeks with the team autonomous and not asking for help again on the same topic.

### Complicated-subsystem team
- Stream-aligned teams use the pricing engine through its API without consulting the expert team in 95% of cases.
- Zero incidents in consuming teams caused by subsystem contract changes this quarter.

### Interaction mode transitions
Specific to Team Topologies: an OKR can measure when a capability moves from collaboration to X-as-a-Service.
- Teams that started in collaboration mode with the platform team consume the capability as a service, with no pairing sessions, in half the time it took the first team.

### Team Topologies counter-examples

Each fails the test: the subject is the serving team or the system, or the number can rise with no change in anyone's behavior.
- **"Migrate everything to Kubernetes"**, **"Launch the developer portal"**. Outputs. Ask what developers do differently once these exist; that behavior is the key result and the migration is a hypothesis.
- **"99.9% platform availability"**. System metric. It is a key result only when translated into behavior (teams stop building workarounds or fallback infrastructure), or tracked as business-as-usual health.
- **"Number of services running on the platform"**. Vanity metric. It rises when teams are forced onto the platform, hiding whether it creates value.
- **"Mandate that all teams adopt the pipeline by Q3"**. Measures compliance. Voluntary adoption is the honest signal; a mandate masks it.
- **"Run 10 workshops on observability"**. Team activity. What counts is what attendees do afterward.
- **"Reduce platform team ticket backlog by 50%"**. Can be reached by rejecting tickets or making requests harder to file. The behavior to measure is teams not needing to file them.
- **"Platform NPS above 50"**. Opinion, not behavior. Pair it with what teams do (renew use, recommend to other teams, adopt voluntarily) before trusting it.
- **"Enabling team delivers 5 coaching engagements"**. Activity, and it can reward dependence. Success is the coached team becoming autonomous.
- **Individual OKRs for platform engineers** (e.g. "Alice ships 3 features"). Breaks collaboration and invites sandbagging. OKRs stay at team level.

## Objective patterns

An objective is qualitative, inspirational, time-bound, and inverts an obstacle. Compare:
- "Increase retention by 10%" is a key result with a number; it does not belong in the objective.
- "Make onboarding something customers finish on their own, by Q3" is an objective.
